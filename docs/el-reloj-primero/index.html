The short version

Against TrainingPeaks

We're ahead on execution. We have our own Watch app for every format (runs, strength, EMOM, HYROX stations, ergs), live PM5 and treadmill control, HYROX and strength formats they don't have, a readiness score, and coach AI built on the coach's own method.

We're behind on analytics and reach. They price load from power, draw best-effort curves, detect new thresholds, let you tune the fitness chart, plan the season, send workouts to five watch brands two weeks ahead, run on Android and speak nine languages.

Their weak side is ours. They market themselves for HYROX, but rest, tempo and RIR are free text, there's no EMOM, AMRAP or For Time, and strength never reaches a watch. Garmin now owns them, so a first-class Apple Watch experience is unlikely to come from them.

Our Watch today

It's the phone's sensor, not the owner of the session. In nine sessions out of ten the phone runs the clock, and the Watch shows what the phone sends.

  • Motion capture (reps, bar speed) runs only when the Watch trains alone.
  • It records no GPS route, asks no RPE, and holds only today's plan.
  • Its motion recordings never leave the phone.

Apple's own guidance is the opposite. At WWDC25 Apple said to start the workout on the Watch "to get all available metrics" and mirror it to the iPhone. The design below does exactly that, then turns on everything the hardware can measure.

The architecture: the Watch owns the session

Today the phone runs the clock and the Watch is a sensor with a screen. We flip it. The Watch already runs the whole engine when it trains alone; it becomes the owner every time. Three roles:

Apple Watch · owns the session

  • Runs the engine (the same shared code it runs today when training alone) and the clock. Apple never suspends it during a workout.
  • Reads every sensor, and connects to machines over Bluetooth itself: up to two, e.g. a PM5 and a chest strap.
  • Writes every sample to its own storage first, then sends.
  • Holds 7 days of plan, so a morning run needs no phone.
  • Uploads itself over Wi-Fi or cellular, or through the phone when it's near.

iPhone · second screen and hub

  • Shows the Watch's session in full and sends commands: next, pause, load typed, notes.
  • Apple relaunches it in the background when the Watch starts, and reconnects it by itself after a dropout.
  • Lock Screen Live Activity. Bridges a third machine when the Watch's two Bluetooth slots are full.
  • Without a Watch it runs the session itself (iOS 26 workout session). That's the fallback, never the default.

Server · the record

  • Accepts every finished workout. If the coach removed the session, it's stored as off-plan and flagged. This is already live in PR #191.
  • Identifies every session by the id the Watch gave it, so a repeated upload never duplicates.
  • Joins the Watch's and phone's parts of the same session.
  • Computes every analysis on the page above.

Six rules the whole design keeps

  1. One owner. While a Watch records, the phone never runs its own clock. The rest timer, the EMOM minute and the station clock all come from the Watch.
  2. Only Apple's events change the link. No timer ever ends, relaunches or discards anything. This keeps the rule from the last rewrite (FH-56), which was right.
  3. Every message names its session. Each carries the session id and a sequence number. Totals travel instead of increments, so a lost message costs nothing. An "end" only ends its own session, which removes the stale-end bug at the root.
  4. Write first, send second. Nothing exists only in memory or only in transit. A finished session waits on the Watch until the server confirms it, with no expiry.
  5. Either side can start or join. Starting on the Watch is a first-class path. Starting on the phone asks the Watch to start. If the Watch doesn't answer, the phone shows «Reintentar» and the Watch shows «Unirse».
  6. One honest status on both screens. Every status comes from a fact: joined, watch app not installed, phone out of range. A silence changes what's shown, never what happens.

Where it can still fail

Apple's limits we can't remove

  • A Watch that's off, locked or charging can't be launched from the phone. That's why starting on the Watch must be first-class.
  • One workout app at a time on the Watch.
  • Two Bluetooth connections for our app on the Watch.
  • Running form is documented for outdoor runs only.
  • Apple Health can't store a HYROX race as one workout with mixed station types. Health gets one functional workout; our own data keeps every leg.
  • Apple blocks WebSockets on the Watch. Anything live for the coach goes over plain web requests.

Not proven until a device runs it

  • A PM5 connected from the Watch through a full session.
  • The size limit of messages between Watch and phone. Apple doesn't document it, so the session state must stay compact.
  • Battery on a 90-minute HYROX simulation.
  • One correction to the audit: Apple lists the workout background mode as Watch-only. The audit's fix of "declare it on the phone" wouldn't work. Watch-first makes it unnecessary.
  • Rep counting: about 96% of sets are within ±1 rep in published studies. Sled push and farmers carry can't be recognised from the wrist, so the race order times them.

Hardware tiers

  • High-rate motion (800 Hz): Series 8 / Ultra and later. Older Watches get 100 Hz, which is what the published studies use.
  • Double tap: Series 9 / Ultra 2 and later.
  • Upload without the phone: Wi-Fi anywhere; away from Wi-Fi only on cellular Watches.
  • watchOS 27 features (native zones, RMSSD variability): reportedly Series 9, SE 3, Ultra 2 and later. That list comes from press reports, not Apple.

Process and market

  • None of this is proven until a Mac compiles it and it runs on your iPhone and Watch. That's why phase 0 comes first.
  • Garmin now owns TrainingPeaks. An unverified headline says Garmin paused developer API access in September, and our Garmin sync depends on that API.
  • Android athletes get none of the Watch edge.

Build order

Each phase has an exit test run on your devices. A phase isn't done because the code compiles.

Phase 0 · Proof before code

Needs you: a Mac build service, and an hour of device tests per phase
  • A Mac builds the phone and Watch apps and runs the 1,884 tests on every change. A red build can't merge.
  • Crash and hang reports, plus a log of failed saves and link events, sent to our server.
  • A hidden «Diagnóstico del reloj» screen showing the last link events from both devices.

Exit: the first device run is logged and readable from here.

Phase 1 · Nothing is ever lost

  • The Watch writes each sample to storage before sending anything.
  • A finished session waits in an outbox with receipts and no expiry.
  • Athletes stay signed in: no more hard 30-day expiry and logout loops.
  • Motion files upload, with a consent screen.
  • The server side is already done: it never rejects a finished workout.

Exit: the audit's kill tests (force-quit, airplane mode, expired session, coach deletes mid-workout) all end with the workout on the server.

Phase 2 · The Watch owns the session

  • Flip the owner. The phone becomes a second screen and remote.
  • Named messages, start or join from either side, one honest status.
  • 7 days of plan on the Watch. The Watch uploads by itself.
  • RPE on the crown. Double tap, the crown and Water Lock instead of tap-anywhere.
  • The screens for every link state are designed first as proposal screens in «el doble», for your sign-off, per the UX rule.

Exit: the 14 sessions above pass on your devices.

Phase 3 · Measure everything

  • Route, elevation, cadence and running form from the Watch.
  • Motion on in every session, with rep counting and bar speed on by default and shown with their confidence.
  • HYROX splits saved leg by leg. PM5 and strap connected from the Watch. Heart-rate recovery after every session.
  • The recovery data we skip today: respiratory rate, blood oxygen, wrist temperature, sleep stages.

Exit: every row of the measurement table reads "yes" on a real session.

Phase 4 · Analytics level with TrainingPeaks

Web and server work, so it can run alongside phases 0–3 and be tested here
  • Load from power on the ergs; fitness-chart constants as the coach's data.
  • Grade-adjusted pace, efficiency and intensity factor.
  • Best-effort curves from the second-by-second data. Threshold detection in both directions.
  • Weekly load targets with a projected fitness line.
  • Structured workouts to Garmin, COROS, Polar and Suunto 14 days ahead.
  • A session explorer.

Exit: no row left "Behind" in the TrainingPeaks table.

Phase 5 · Past them

  • A live board for the box: every athlete's heart rate, zone and station on a screen.
  • The coach's AI adapts on what was measured (bar speed, stations, readiness), not only on what was typed.
  • A movement-recognition model trained on our own recordings. That's why phase 1 starts collecting them.
